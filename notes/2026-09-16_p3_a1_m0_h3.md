# 2026-09-16 — P3: A1 extended to M0, H3 gets a real (positive) test

## What was built

Wired M0 (`FedAvgSequential`, family F1) into `shadow_runner.py`'s `METHOD_REGISTRY`. M4/M8's A1
scoring reads one ledger record directly, per target, per round; M0 needed real new machinery because
its released `MODEL_DELTA` is one contribution to an *ever-changing* global model, not a one-shot
per-target statistic. `_reconstruct_running_w(ledger, n_rounds, feature_dim, n_classes)` replays M0's
own FedAvg aggregation (`methods/m0_fedavg.py::fit_task`'s weighted average of per-client deltas)
using only ledger-visible fields (`payload`, `n_touched`) — a legitimate honest-but-curious-server
reconstruction (CLAUDE.md non-negotiable #1: attacks only ever consume the ledger, never internal
method state), not a shortcut through `self.W`. A target's score at round T is the logit margin
(true-class logit minus the best competing logit) under the reconstructed round-T global model.

Config matches the real TAB05 sweep (`run_utility_baseline.py`): `local_epochs=30, lr=0.5` — the
headline M0 config, not an attack-specific weakening. 2 new tests (`test_m0_trajectory_and_last_round_are_not_identical`
pins the property this whole exercise was for), 158/158 total, ruff clean.

Real run: 4,096 shadows, CIFAR-100, same 500-target pool and population-resampling design as M4/M8
(`notes/2026-09-16_p3_a1_population_resampling.md`). One real infrastructure hiccup: 2 of 32 array
tasks (indices 5, 6) died silently mid-run (empty logs, no traceback — looks like a transient
node/scheduler issue, not a code bug) after writing only their first 8-shadow wave each. Resumability
worked exactly as designed: resubmitting just those two indices (`-J 5-6`) backfilled the missing 240
shadows for free, skipping the 16 already written. `results/a1_lira_cifar100_m0_fedavg.csv`.

## The result: a real, positive test of H3

H3: *TPR@1%FPR under `V_full` (the full transcript) substantially exceeds `V_final`/checkpoint-only,
growing with the number of observed rounds.* M4/M8 (each releases evidence once per target) made this
comparison an algebraic identity — `trajectory` (sums per-round log-LR across every released round)
and `last_round` (uses only the latest) came back bit-for-bit equal, a real but negative/degenerate
result reported honestly rather than forced. **M0 is the method that actually tests it, and the
prediction holds:**

| elapsed (T−k) | AUC (`trajectory`) | AUC (`last_round`) | gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.560 | 0.560 | 0.000 (only one round observed, arms are identical by construction) |
| 1 | 0.570 | 0.539 | 0.031 |
| 2 | 0.573 | 0.534 | 0.039 |
| 5 | 0.569 | 0.533 | 0.037 |
| 8 | 0.557 | 0.523 | 0.035 |
| 9 | 0.555 | 0.520 | 0.035 |

(`n_pos`≈`n_neg` from ~204k at elapsed=0 down to ~20k at elapsed=9 — well-powered throughout; full
table in the CSV.) The gap opens immediately at elapsed=1 and stays open (roughly flat, not shrinking)
out to elapsed=9 — the full transcript retains a persistent ~0.03-0.04 AUC advantage over the latest
checkpoint alone, at every horizon tested. **This is real evidence for H3's "the transcript is worth
more than the checkpoint" claim**, with the caveat that the advantage plateaus rather than growing like
`Θ(√(#observations))` as H3's stronger quantitative form speculated — worth noting precisely rather
than overclaiming the exact growth rate.

## A second, unplanned finding: this is also evidence for the paper's central thesis

Both `trajectory` and `last_round` **decay toward chance (AUC→0.5)** as elapsed grows — `trajectory`
0.573→0.555, `last_round` 0.534→0.520, both trending down past their early peak. This is the opposite
of M4/M8's completely flat curves (`notes/2026-09-16_p3_a1_lira_results.md`): M0 has no anti-forgetting
mechanism, so ordinary SGD drift from later tasks' gradients gradually erases the released delta's
individual influence on the global weight, and the individual-level membership signal decays with it.

This is not what A1/M0 was built to test — it fell out of building H3's real test — but it is directly
on-point for the whole paper's thesis (a method's forgetting mechanism doubles as its privacy-decay
mechanism, and the two are supposed to be *coupled*): the one method with **no** retention mechanism
shows **decaying** individual leakage; the two methods with retention mechanisms (M4's prototype
carry-forward, M8's running Gram sum) show **flat, non-decaying** leakage. That is exactly H2/C1's
predicted contrast, now demonstrated with a real calibrated membership attack (A1), not just the direct
statistic-reading of A6. Should be flagged prominently when H2's real dose-response sweep gets built —
this is a clean, cheap, already-in-hand qualitative preview of the correlation H2 needs to establish
quantitatively across many more methods.
