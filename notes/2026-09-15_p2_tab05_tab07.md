# 2026-09-15 — P2: TAB05 (real utility baselines) and TAB07 (reproduction gap)

## TAB05 — real per-dataset results, 126 runs (7 methods x 6 dataset/n_tasks configs x 3 seeds)

Ran `code/scripts/run_utility_baseline.py` as a chunked PBS array job
(`code/scripts/pbs/utility_sweep_array.pbs`, 13 tasks x ~10 configs each, `batch` queue, no
contention — unlike the GPU queue, this ran to completion in well under an hour) against the real
extracted CIFAR-100/ImageNet-R/CUB-200/Camelyon17 features. Collected into
`results/tab05_utility_baselines.csv` via `collect_tab05.py`. One real bug caught and fixed along
the way: the collector's `RUNS_DIR.glob("*.json")` also matched the `*.json.meta.json` provenance
sidecars `provenance.finalize` writes next to each result, causing a `KeyError` — fixed by excluding
`.meta.json` explicitly.

All 126 rows are plausible: no NaNs, accuracies in sensible ranges, and the qualitative pattern the
whole project is built around shows up immediately in the raw utility numbers even before any attack
runs — M0 (no retention mechanism) has clearly negative BWT (-0.18 to -0.36, real forgetting) on
every dataset; M8 (exact closed-form accumulation) has the best accuracy almost everywhere and near-
zero BWT; M4 (nearest-class-mean, no learned head) is a visibly weaker classifier especially on
harder/more-class datasets (ImageNet-R: 0.33). A few methods (M1, M2, M5) show *positive* BWT on some
datasets — plausible for replay/distillation methods where old-class accuracy can be reinforced above
its immediate post-training value by later rounds' replay, not obviously a bug, but worth a second
look once the attack suite (P3) starts using these same runs.

## TAB07 — reproduction gap (H12)

Looked up real published numbers via WebSearch/WebFetch rather than recalling them from memory —
recall risk was real here (several of these papers, e.g. ICLR'25's Hybrid Replay, are recent enough
that precise numeric recall would have been unreliable). Got solid, cited numbers for GLFC (M1),
TARGET (M2, three independent papers report three different numbers for it — 0.363/0.713/0.500 under
three different protocols, which is itself a clean, citable instance of H12's reproduction-attrition
claim), PILoRA (M4, but only as an upper reference since we implement its prototype half only, not
the LoRA half), and Hybrid Replay (M5). Could **not** extract FOT's (M3) exact Split-CIFAR100 number
despite three separate attempts (arXiv PDF, ICLR proceedings PDF, OpenReview PDF all resisted
automated extraction) — recorded that honestly as an open follow-up in the CSV's `note` column rather
than guessing a plausible-looking number. M0 and M8 have no single published number to compare
against by design (M0 is a generic baseline, not one paper's method; M8 is our own reimplementation
target with no independent public benchmark entry) — both marked `n/a`, not silently omitted.

**The headline finding, and the reason to read the gap column carefully rather than at face value**:
our reimplementations of M1/M2/M5 score 14-49 points *higher* than every published number we found
for them, on the SAME dataset (CIFAR-100) and a comparable task count. This is **not** evidence our
reimplementations are better at continual learning than the original methods — it is almost certainly
a backbone confound. Every method in this harness sits on top of a **frozen, ImageNet-21k-pretrained
ViT-B/16** (RESEARCH_PLAN.md's whole "cacheable methods on frozen features" architecture), whereas the
original GLFC/TARGET/Hybrid Replay papers train a ResNet18 substantially from scratch inside the
federated continual loop — a far weaker starting representation. A strong-enough frozen backbone with
even a naively-trained linear head is well known in the continual-learning literature to outperform
weaker-backbone methods with sophisticated anti-forgetting machinery (this is precisely the premise
that made L2P/DualPrompt/C²Prompt-style "frozen ViT + light adapter" methods the current SOTA family
that RESEARCH_PLAN.md itself cites). **The gap column measures "our harness vs. their harness," not
"our implementation quality vs. their implementation quality."** M4 is the one method that goes the
other way (-0.127, we score *lower*) precisely because it's missing PILoRA's LoRA half — the one part
of that method that would let the backbone adapt at all — which is independent, structural evidence
for the same underlying point: the frozen-backbone choice is doing a lot of the work in this table,
in both directions.

**What this means for the paper**: TAB07's gap numbers should be presented with this caveat
front-and-center, not as a footnote — otherwise a reviewer reads "we beat published SOTA by 15-49
points" and (correctly) doesn't believe it, or worse, believes it and dismisses the paper's rigor once
they notice the backbone difference themselves. The honest framing is closer to: "under a matched,
frozen-ViT-B/16 protocol used identically across every method in our audit (necessary so that later
leakage comparisons isolate the *retention mechanism* rather than confounding it with backbone
capacity), our reimplementations land at X; the original papers' from-scratch-backbone numbers are Y;
the two are not directly comparable, and the gap is dominated by that protocol difference, not by
reimplementation fidelity." This is arguably a *more* interesting methodological point than a clean
apples-to-apples reproduction would have been, but it needs to be stated, not discovered by a reviewer.
